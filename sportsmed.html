PRICE: Crutch Fitting

Crutches Explained

Proper crutch fitting and use are essential for safety and comfort. The crutches should be adjusted to the user’s height, allowing slight elbow bend and space under the armpits. Different weight-bearing types determine how much weight can be placed on the injured leg. When walking or using stairs, balance, posture, and awareness of the surroundings are key to preventing falls or strain.

Sizing and Fitting

  • Have the athlete stand tall with feet together and hands relaxed by their sides
  • Position crutch tips 6” from outer part of the shoes and 2” in front of toes
  • Adjust underarm brace height to a 2-3 finger widths below the anterior fold of the axilla, allowing 20-30 degree bend of the elbow

Using Crutches

  • Non-Weight Bearing (NWB): do not apply any weight through involved leg
    • Straighten the elbows and pull the underarm brace firmly against the side of chest on ribs
    • Lean forward and swing the involved leg followed by the uninvolved leg
    • After moving, athlete recovers the crutches and repeats
  • Partial Weight Bearing (PWB or Crutch Walking: allow a maximum of 50% body weight to be applied to the involved leg
    • Crutch Walking - place more force on crutches - involved leg first, then uninvolved leg

Stairs

  • Upstairs: balance on crutches, step upward with the uninvolved leg, follow with crutches
  • Downstairs: balance on involved leg, place crutches on lower step, follow with the uninvolved leg

PRICE: Vacuum Splint

Vacuum Splint Explained

Vacuum splinting is used to immobilize and stabilize injuries such as fractures or sprains. The splint should be formed and secured around the injured area while maintaining manual stabilization. Air is removed with a vacuum pump until the splint becomes firm, then the pinch clamp is closed to hold its shape. Throughout the process, circulation, movement, and sensation (PMS) should be checked to ensure the splint is snug but not too tight and the limb remains in a comfortable position.

When to Use

  • Used to immobilize and stabilize injuries and protect them
  • Includes fractures, sprains, etc.

Application

  • Choose the correct size splint for the injury
  • Keep manual stabilization until immobilization is complete
  • Shape the vacuum splint around the injured area
  • Secure it gently with Velcro straps
  • Make sure the pinch clamp is open
  • Connect the splint to the vacuum pump
  • Pump air out until the splint becomes rigid
  • Close the pinch clamp, then remove the pump
  • Recheck straps for snugness and assess PMS

Checking PMS/CMS

  • Circulation/Pulse: Check pulse, blood pressure, capillary refill test
  • Motor: Ask the patient to move parts of their body (fingers, toes…)
  • Sensation: Lightly touch the area for feedback
  • Normal = healthy circulation and nerves
  • Abnomal = conditions such as nerve damage, stroke, or lack of blood flow
  • Check PMS/CMS BEFORE and AFTER applying splint

PRICE: Cryotherapy

Cryotherapy Explained

Tissue cooling, or cryotherapy, decreases tissue temperature and metabolic rate, helping injured tissue survive by reducing oxygen demand and limiting further damage. It also decreases pain by slowing nerve conduction and activating the gate control theory, where cold sensations override pain signals. Cooling causes vasoconstriction, reducing bleeding, swelling, and inflammation—especially effective during the acute phase of injury. Common methods include ice massage, cold packs, ice bags, cold whirlpools, ice towels, and cold compression units, each typically applied for 5–20 minutes depending on the area and technique.

Ice Bags

  • Equipment
    • Plastic bags
    • Ice machine, water, electricity
    • Moist towels (optional)
    • Elastic bandage or plastic wrap (hold and compression)
    • Salt solution or water (optional) - more thermal energy
  • Treatment Procedure
    • Position athlete
    • Pack must be placed ON SKIN
    • Secure in place with elastic bandage or plastic wrap
    • 20’
  • Making an Ice Bag
    • Use a plastic bag and scoop ice into the bag making a ball shape
    • Take all the air out of the bag and then twist the bag
    • Tie a knot about 2 inches above the ball of ice, then flatten the ball into 1 inch thick

Cold Whirlpool

  • Equipment
    • Whirpool
    • Water
    • Ice
    • Electricity with GFI
    • Towels
  • Treatment Procedure
    • Fill with water and ice until 50-60 degrees
    • Ice must be melted
    • Total body immersion 65-80 degrees
    • 5-20’

Ice Towel

  • Wet towels are more effective at cooling than dry towels
  • Equipment
      • Medium sized towels
    • Ice
    • Water
  • Treatment Procedure
      • Position athlete in comfort
    • Expose treatment area
    • Dry towel to protect clothes and catch water
    • Wet towel and fill w/ ice
    • Fold ice in towel and place on treatment area
    • 20’

Ice Massage

  • Best indicated for localized pain
  • Equipment
    • CryoCup
    • 6-8 oz cup with tongue depressor
    • Freezer, electricity, water source
    • Towel
  • Treatment Procedure
    • Athlete position of comfort with towel beneath area
    • Self treatment if possible
    • Circular motion - half overlap
    • Longitudinal strokes - half overlap
    • Smooth rough edges with hand
    • Touch treatment area with cold hand to introduce
    • Timer set for 7’ give or take

PRICE: Compression

Compression Explained

Purpose

  • Provides support and compression to injured areas
  • Reduces swelling (edema) and limits movement to prevent further injury
  • Secures pads, ice packs, splints, or dressings in place
  • Protects the area during recovery or activity

Types of Injuries

  • Sprains (ligament injuries)
  • Strains (muscle/tendon injuries)
  • Contusions (bruises)
  • Hematomas (localized bleeding/swelling)
  • Bursitis (inflamed bursa)

Elbow Spiral Wrap

  • Contract all muscles on arm
  • Begin wrapping below the elbow (forearm)
  • Anchor the bandage with one to two turns around the forearm
  • Continue wrapping upward in a spiral, overlapping each turn by half
  • Maintain moderate tension—not too tight
  • Finish with one to two turns above the elbow (upper arm)
  • Secure the end with tape, clips, or tuck-in
  • Check CMS (capillary refill) after wrapping

Ankle Spica Wrap

  • Have the athlete sit or lie down with the foot at a 90° angle
  • Start wrapping around the instep or base of the foot, anchoring the bandage
  • Bring the bandage up over the ankle joint and around the leg
  • Cross the bandage back down and around the foot in a figure-8 pattern
  • Repeat this pattern several times, overlapping by half each turn, forming a V (spica) shape at the ankle
  • Maintain even pressure and ensure full coverage of the joint
  • Secure the end with tape or clips
  • Recheck PMS (pulse, motor, sensation) to confirm proper circulation

Horseshoe

  • Focal pressure
  • Measure ankle posterior to anterior for width
  • 4-6 inches for length
  • Cut bottom corners to shape a ‘U’
  • Place under malleolus
  • Use an elastic bandage (either spiral or spica)

PRICE: Elevation

Elevation Explained

Elevation is used to help reduce swelling and pain after an injury by raising the injured area above the level of the heart.

Purpose

  • Decreases swelling (edema): Gravity helps drain excess fluid away from the injured area and back toward the heart
  • Reduces internal bleeding: Less blood flow collects around the injury
  • Lowers pressure: Decreasing fluid buildup relieves pressure on tissues and nerves, reducing pain
  • Improves healing: Less swelling allows better oxygen and nutrient delivery to the injured tissues
