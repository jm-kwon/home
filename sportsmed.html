PRICE: Crutch Fitting

Crutches Explained

Proper crutch fitting and use are essential for safety and comfort. The crutches should be adjusted to the user’s height, allowing slight elbow bend and space under the armpits. Different weight-bearing types determine how much weight can be placed on the injured leg. When walking or using stairs, balance, posture, and awareness of the surroundings are key to preventing falls or strain.

Sizing and Fitting

  • Have the athlete stand tall with feet together and hands relaxed by their sides
  • Position crutch tips 6” from outer part of the shoes and 2” in front of toes
  • Adjust underarm brace height to a 2-3 finger widths below the anterior fold of the axilla, allowing 20-30 degree bend of the elbow

Using Crutches

  • Non-Weight Bearing (NWB): do not apply any weight through involved leg
    • Straighten the elbows and pull the underarm brace firmly against the side of chest on ribs
    • Lean forward and swing the involved leg followed by the uninvolved leg
    • After moving, athlete recovers the crutches and repeats
  • Partial Weight Bearing (PWB or Crutch Walking: allow a maximum of 50% body weight to be applied to the involved leg
    • Crutch Walking - place more force on crutches - involved leg first, then uninvolved leg

Stairs

  • Upstairs: balance on crutches, step upward with the uninvolved leg, follow with crutches
  • Downstairs: balance on involved leg, place crutches on lower step, follow with the uninvolved leg

PRICE: Vacuum Splint

Vacuum Splint Explained

Vacuum splinting is used to immobilize and stabilize injuries such as fractures or sprains. The splint should be formed and secured around the injured area while maintaining manual stabilization. Air is removed with a vacuum pump until the splint becomes firm, then the pinch clamp is closed to hold its shape. Throughout the process, circulation, movement, and sensation (PMS) should be checked to ensure the splint is snug but not too tight and the limb remains in a comfortable position.

When to Use

  • Used to immobilize and stabilize injuries and protect them
  • Includes fractures, sprains, etc.

Application

  • Choose the correct size splint for the injury
  • Keep manual stabilization until immobilization is complete
  • Shape the vacuum splint around the injured area
  • Secure it gently with Velcro straps
  • Make sure the pinch clamp is open
  • Connect the splint to the vacuum pump
  • Pump air out until the splint becomes rigid
  • Close the pinch clamp, then remove the pump
  • Recheck straps for snugness and assess PMS

Checking PMS/CMS

  • Circulation/Pulse: Check pulse, blood pressure, capillary refill test
  • Motor: Ask the patient to move parts of their body (fingers, toes…)
  • Sensation: Lightly touch the area for feedback
  • Normal = healthy circulation and nerves
  • Abnomal = conditions such as nerve damage, stroke, or lack of blood flow
  • Check PMS/CMS BEFORE and AFTER applying splint

PRICE: Cryotherapy

Cryotherapy Explained

Tissue cooling, or cryotherapy, decreases tissue temperature and metabolic rate, helping injured tissue survive by reducing oxygen demand and limiting further damage. It also decreases pain by slowing nerve conduction and activating the gate control theory, where cold sensations override pain signals. Cooling causes vasoconstriction, reducing bleeding, swelling, and inflammation—especially effective during the acute phase of injury. Common methods include ice massage, cold packs, ice bags, cold whirlpools, ice towels, and cold compression units, each typically applied for 5–20 minutes depending on the area and technique.

Ice Bags

  • Equipment
    • Plastic bags
    • Ice machine, water, electricity
    • Moist towels (optional)
    • Elastic bandage or plastic wrap (hold and compression)
    • Salt solution or water (optional) - more thermal energy
  • Treatment Procedure
    • Position athlete
    • Pack must be placed ON SKIN
    • Secure in place with elastic bandage or plastic wrap
    • 20’
  • Making an Ice Bag
    • Use a plastic bag and scoop ice into the bag making a ball shape
    • Take all the air out of the bag and then twist the bag
    • Tie a knot about 2 inches above the ball of ice, then flatten the ball into 1 inch thick

Cold Whirlpool

  • Equipment
    • Whirpool
    • Water
    • Ice
    • Electricity with GFI
    • Towels
  • Treatment Procedure
    • Fill with water and ice until 50-60 degrees
    • Ice must be melted
    • Total body immersion 65-80 degrees
    • 5-20’

Ice Towel

  • Wet towels are more effective at cooling than dry towels
  • Equipment
      • Medium sized towels
    • Ice
    • Water
  • Treatment Procedure
      • Position athlete in comfort
    • Expose treatment area
    • Dry towel to protect clothes and catch water
    • Wet towel and fill w/ ice
    • Fold ice in towel and place on treatment area
    • 20’

Ice Massage

  • Best indicated for localized pain
  • Equipment
    • CryoCup
    • 6-8 oz cup with tongue depressor
    • Freezer, electricity, water source
    • Towel
  • Treatment Procedure
    • Athlete position of comfort with towel beneath area
    • Self treatment if possible
    • Circular motion - half overlap
    • Longitudinal strokes - half overlap
    • Smooth rough edges with hand
    • Touch treatment area with cold hand to introduce
    • Timer set for 7’ give or take

PRICE: Compression

Compression Explained

Purpose

  • Provides support and compression to injured areas
  • Reduces swelling (edema) and limits movement to prevent further injury
  • Secures pads, ice packs, splints, or dressings in place
  • Protects the area during recovery or activity

Types of Injuries

  • Sprains (ligament injuries)
  • Strains (muscle/tendon injuries)
  • Contusions (bruises)
  • Hematomas (localized bleeding/swelling)
  • Bursitis (inflamed bursa)

Elbow Spiral Wrap

  • Contract all muscles on arm
  • Begin wrapping below the elbow (forearm)
  • Anchor the bandage with one to two turns around the forearm
  • Continue wrapping upward in a spiral, overlapping each turn by half
  • Maintain moderate tension—not too tight
  • Finish with one to two turns above the elbow (upper arm)
  • Secure the end with tape, clips, or tuck-in
  • Check CMS (capillary refill) after wrapping

Ankle Spica Wrap

  • Have the athlete sit or lie down with the foot at a 90° angle
  • Start wrapping around the instep or base of the foot, anchoring the bandage
  • Bring the bandage up over the ankle joint and around the leg
  • Cross the bandage back down and around the foot in a figure-8 pattern
  • Repeat this pattern several times, overlapping by half each turn, forming a V (spica) shape at the ankle
  • Maintain even pressure and ensure full coverage of the joint
  • Secure the end with tape or clips
  • Recheck PMS (pulse, motor, sensation) to confirm proper circulation

Horseshoe

  • Focal pressure
  • Measure ankle posterior to anterior for width
  • 4-6 inches for length
  • Cut bottom corners to shape a ‘U’
  • Place under malleolus
  • Use an elastic bandage (either spiral or spica)

PRICE: Elevation

Elevation Explained

Elevation is used to help reduce swelling and pain after an injury by raising the injured area above the level of the heart.

Purpose

  • Decreases swelling (edema): Gravity helps drain excess fluid away from the injured area and back toward the heart
  • Reduces internal bleeding: Less blood flow collects around the injury
  • Lowers pressure: Decreasing fluid buildup relieves pressure on tissues and nerves, reducing pain
  • Improves healing: Less swelling allows better oxygen and nutrient delivery to the injured tissues

Foot and Ankle Taping: Turf Toe

Turf Toe Explained

Sprain of first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint caused by hyperextension of the big toe

Uses of Turf Toe Taping

Before competition, practice, active use

Purpose of Turf Toe Taping

To prevent hyperextension further injury, reduce pain, and provide support of the plantar aspect of the foot to prevent MTP from extension

Components

  • Tuff-Skin (tape adherent)
  • 1’ tape
  • 1 ½’ tape

Procedure

  • Apply tape adherent
  • Place the athlete into dorsiflexion, with toe flexion
  • First anchor on midarch with 1 ½’ tape and second on the 1st proxima phalanx with 1’ tape
  • 3-4 support strips on the plantar side from anchor to anchor with half overlap (medial to lateral) - 1’ tape
  • Cover strips 1 ½’ tape - apply with pressure on the bottom, looser on top
  • Fin is optional

Foot and Ankle Taping: Achilles Tendon

Achilles Tendon Tape Explained

Provide support to the calf (gastro, soleus, achilles tendon complex) and decrease dorsiflexion ROM

Uses of Achilles Tendon Tape

During practices, competitions, achilles tendonitis

Achilles Tendon Tape Purpose

Decreases force applied to Achilles tendon and surrounding structures and for support thereby reducing pain

Components

  • Tuff-Skin (tape adherent)
  • 2" elastic adhesive tape
  • 1 ½’ tape

Procedure

  • Avoid prewrap if possible
  • Position athlete in a relaxed, neutral position (slight plantar flexion)
  • Apply 3 Anchors similar to ankle taping
  • On the posterior lower leg, apply 2” elastic adhesive tape without a stretch on anchor
  • Secure down with 1 ½ “ tape on anchor
  • Stretch elastic adhesive tape in the middle and end on the distal anchor without a stretch
  • Cut tape with scissors and secure down with 1 ½ “ tape on distal anchor
  • Repeat once more
  • Once second support tape is applied, place athlete into dorsiflexion
  • Apply cover strips proximal to distal similar to horseshoes
  • Finish with figure 8

Foot and Ankle Taping: Kinesiotape

Kinesiotape Explained

A taping technique created by Dr. Kenzo Kase to help heal injuries, alleviate pain, and support muscles while still allowing normal movement

Uses of Kinesiotape

Pain reduction, controlling edema, and still allowing freedom of movement

Kinesiotape Purpose

Provides elastic support, helps lymphatic drainage, reduces edema, and decreases pain by lifting the skin

Procedure: Ankle Sprain

  • Creating Fan Strip
    • Cut required length of tape from the roll (measure by placing on posterior-lateral side from lateral malleolus to the cuneiform bones
    • Round one end to form the base (about 4–5 cm or square shape)
    • Cut 3–4 longitudinal strips into the tape, forming four equal strips
    • Fold in half while cutting to maintain equal width
    • Round the corners of all strips to improve adhesion
    • Hold the base and activate the adhesive by rubbing
  • Applying Kinesiotape
    • Prep skin by ensuring the skin is dry, clean, and use of tape adherent
    • Start on posterior-lateral side from lateral malleolus
    • Do not touch the adhesive part of the tape
    • Apply each strip with 10-20% stretch with:
    • 1st: proximal end of 1st metatarsal
    • 2nd: proximal 2nd metatarsal
    • 3rd: proximal 5th metatarsal
    • 4th: lateral side of 5th metatarsal
    • Repeat second fan on medial side, directing toward the lateral aspect of the foot
  • Remain on for 3-5 days

Knee Goniometry

Goniometer Explained

A goniometer is a medical device used to measure the ROM of a joint, allowing the examiner to evaluate rehabilitation, determine flexibility of joints, and detect abnormalities

Procedure of Goniometer Use

  • Place center of the goniometer at the center of the joint
  • Line the stationary arm to the fixed part of the joint
  • Align the moving arm to the other segment of the joint, holding this part in a neutral position
  • Once goniometer is in place, ask pt to move the segment
  • Read the measurement correctly Not always the number that is shown on the goniometer as it is relative!

Normal Ranges

  • Knee Flexion: 130-140°
  • Knee Extension: 0-10°
AROM Knee Flexion Supine ~ 129°
AROM Knee Flexion Prone ~ 125°
AROM Knee Extension ~ 1°

Knee Special Tests

Lachman's Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position, with the examiner standing at the pt side
    • One hand on the tibial tuberosity and the other above the femoral condyles
    • Flex patient’s knee to 20-25 degrees
    • Anteriorly translate the tibia while stabilizing the femur
    • Modification: Place a firm object underneath the thigh to help stabilize the femur
  • Indications
    • Anterior tibial translation with no end point
  • Implications
    • ACL Sprain/Tear

Anterior Drawer

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position
    • 45 degree hip flexion and 90 degree knee flexion, with the examiner sitting on the pt feet
    • Palpate hamstrings to ensure pt is relaxed
    • Both hands over tibia with the thumb on the joint line
    • Anteriorly translate the tibia
  • Indications
    • Anterior tibial translation with no end point
  • Implications
    • ACL Sprain/Tear

Posterior Drawer

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position
    • 45 degree hip flexion and 90 degree knee flexion, with the examiner sitting on the pt feet
    • Palpate hamstrings to ensure pt is relaxed
    • Both hands over tibia with the thumb on the joint line
    • Posteriorly translate the tibia
  • Indications
    • Posterior tibial translation with no end point
  • Implications
    • PCL Sprain/Tear

Godfrey's Test / Sag Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position, with the examiner standing at the pt side
    • Lift the lower extremities under the knee and hold them parallel to the table in a 90-90 position
  • Indications
    • Unilateral posterior sag
    • Displaced tibial tuberosity
  • Implications
    • PCL Sprain/Tear

Valgus Stress Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position, with the examiner standing at the pt side
    • Relax the knee and apply valgus force by applying medial pressure to the lateral knee while pushing distal tibia laterally
    • Perform at both 0 degrees and 25 degrees
  • Indications
    • Increased laxity without endpoint
  • Implications
    • MCL Sprain/Tear @ 0 and 25 degrees
    • Other possible structures @ 0 degrees

Varus Stress Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position, with the examiner standing at the pt side
    • Relax the knee and apply varus force by applying lateral pressure to the medial knee while pushing distal tibia medially
    • Peform at both 0 degrees and 25 degrees
  • Indications
    • Increased laxity without end point
  • Implications
    • LCL Sprain/Tear @ 0 and 25 degrees
    • Other possible structures @ 0 degrees

Apley's Compression & Distraction Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into prone position and knee flexion at 90 degrees while the examiner standing at the pt side
    • Apply downward pressure on the calcaneus while internally and externally rotating the tibia
    • Move hand beneath the foot and apply upward force while internally and externally rotating the tibia
  • Indications
    • Pain with compression & reduced to no pain with distraction
    • Pain with distraction
  • Implications
    • Meniscus if there is pain with compression and reduced/no pain with distraction
    • Medial/Lateral Collateral Ligament(s) if there is pain with distraction

Patellar Apprehension Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position with knee extended while the examiner stands at the pt side
    • Put fingers at the medial patella border
    • Ensure knee is relaxed and passively push the patella laterally
  • Indications
    • Apprehension or Pain
  • Implications

Clark's Sign / Patellar Grind Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supine position and knee extended, with examiner standing at pt side

  • Indications

    • Patellar femoral syndrome

Thomas Test

  • Procedure
    • Pt stands on edge of treatment table and lays into supine position, grabbing both knees
    • While holding onto one knee, drop the other leg into relaxed position
    • Examine position and angles of the hip and knee
  • Indications
    • Thigh is above the table; greater than the hip
    • Knee is in extension; less than 90 degrees
    • Hip abduction
    • Hip flexor tightness
    • Rectus femoris tightness
    • TFL/IT Band tightness

90-90 Straight Leg Raise

  • Procedure
    • Pt is in a supinated position with hips and knees at 90 degrees
    • Pt can either actively extend knee while grasping thighs or examiner passively extend the knee until taught
  • Indications
    • Unable to extend the knee within 20 degrees of full knee extension
  • Implications
    • Hamstring muscle tightness

FABER Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supinated position with foot crossed over opposite knee
    • Examiner place one hand on opposing ASIS with the other on the medial aspect of closer knee
    • Apply pressure down
  • Indications
    • Pt unable to lower thigh onto the table
    • Hip joint pain
  • Implications
    • Iliopsoas tightness
    • Hip pathology
    • SI joint pathology

SI Compression Test

  • Procedure
    • Place pt into supinated position
    • Examiner crosses hands to place onto opposing sides of the pelvis
    • Apply pressure to spread the ASIS
  • Indications
    • Pain from the SI joint
  • Implications
    • SI pathology

Trendelenburg Test

  • Procedure
    • Have pt stands with feet spreaded evenly
    • Examiner sits or kneels behind the athlete
    • Place hands on rights and left iliac crests
    • Have pt lift their leg, watching the pelvis
  • Indications
    • Pain through SI joint
  • Implications
    • Pain through SI joint

Ober’s Test

  • Procedure
    • Have pt lie into lateral recumbent position of tested hip w/ hip at 45 and knee at 90
    • Examiner stabilizes the pelvis with one hand while supporting the leg above the knee with the other hand then abducts & extends the hip
    • Let the knee drop
  • Indications
    • Leg does not adduct past parallel
  • Implications
    • IT Band tightness

Noble Compression Test

  • Procedure
    • Pt is supinated with knee flexed
    • Examiner supports knee just above the joint line w/ thumb proximal to lateral femoral epicondyle
    • Apply pressure while moving knee into extension and flexion
  • Indications
    • Pain underneath the thumb as knee approaches 90 degrees in flexion
  • Implications
    • IT Band inflammation

Lower Extremity Stretching

Static Stretching

  • Use as cooldown after training
  • Hold stretch for 10” to 30” for three sets
  • Improves flexibility
Gastrocnemius: With patient standing, place one foot back with knee in extension and heel flat on the ground
Soleus: With patient standing, place one foot back with knee in slight flexion and heel flat on the ground
Tibilais Anterior: With patient standing, place one foot back with toes pointed down and press foot into the ground
Hamstrings: With patient sitting, extend one leg out with the other bent while hinging forward with straight back
Quadriceps: With patient standing, grab ankle and pull knee into flexion, ensuring knees are together
Iliopsoas: With patient lunging, place back knee on the ground while pushing hips forward
Adductors: With patient side-lunging, bend one knee and keep the other extended, putting force on bent side
Abductors: With patient standing, cross one leg over the other and lean sideways
Piriformis: With patient standing, cross one leg on top of other knee and ‘sit’
Gluteus Maximus: With patient standing, pull one knee to chest

Dynamic Stretching

  • Performed before practice
  • Prepare muscles for activity
High Knees: Repeated alternating hip flexion with arms driving from ‘cheek to cheek’
  • Activated Muscles: Iliopsoas, Rectus Femoris
  • Stretched Muscles: Gluteus Maximus, Hamstrings
Butt Kickers: Repeated knee flexion with arms driving from ‘cheek’ to ‘cheek’
  • Activated Muscles: Hamstrings
  • Stretched Muscles: Quadriceps
Frankenstein's: Hip flexion with knee extension (hands on hips to keep straight back)
  • Activated Muscles: Iliopsoas, Rectus Femoris
  • Stretched Muscles: Hamstrings
Medial Leg Swings: Hip adduction, using wall to support
  • Activated Muscles: Adductors
  • Stretched Muscles: Abductors
Lateral Leg Swings: Hip abduction, using wall to support
  • Activated Muscles: Abductors
  • Stretched Muscles: Adductors

PNF Stretching

  • Quadriceps Contract Relax
    • Static: Place pt in prone position, pulling heel toward pt glutes; Hold for 10”
    • Isotonic: Tell pt to straighten their knee, applying a concentric force and allowing pt to slowly move their knee into extension

Wrist, Hand, and Fingers Evaluation

Glide Test

  • Procedure
    • Tell pt to relax with forearm pronated and elbow flexed to 90 degrees
    • Stabilize the distal radius while the other hand grips the proximal carpals
    • Apply sheer forces in palmar, dorsal, radial, and ulnar aspects
  • Indications
    • Pain or laxity
  • Implications
    • Tears or stretches to collateral or intercarpal ligaments
    • Trauma to fibrocartilage complex
    • Capsular stiffness or adhesion if decreased glide

Wrist Valgus/Varus Stress Test

  • Procedure
    • Tell pt to relax with forearm pronated and elbow flexed to 90 degrees
    • Stabilize the forearm while gripping the metacarpals with the other
    • Apply valgus and varus stress by diviating the wrist
  • Indications
    • Pain or laxity
  • Implications
    • Wrist Sprain

Tinel’s Sign

  • Procedure
    • Position pt with hand supinated
    • Tap over the transverse carpal ligament
  • Indications
    • Tingling along the median nerve
  • Implications
    • Carpal Tunnel Syndrome

Phalen’s Test

  • Procedure
    • Ask pt to fully flex their wrists together in front of their body with the dorsal aspect of the hands pressed against the other
    • Hold for 30 seconds to a minute
  • Indications
    • Numbness or tingling in the hands and fingers
  • Implications
    • Median nerve compression

Pinch Grip Test

  • Procedure
    • Position pt in thumb to pointer finger opposition
    • Hold a paper or the examiner also does opposition in betweeen the pt’s fingers and apply pressure by attempting to pull out
  • Indications
    • Paper or examiner’s fingers are easily pulled out
  • Implications
    • Median nerve pathology

Finger Valgus/Varus Stress Test

  • Procedure
    • Have pt relax with the joint being tested to be in finger extension
    • Stabilize the proximal phalanx and grip the distal phalanx to the joint
    • Apply valgus and varus forces
  • Indications
    • Gapping when compared bilaterally and pain
  • Implications
    • Finger joint sprain

Gamekeeper’s Thumb

  • Procedure
    • Pt relax and stabilize the metacarpal
    • Apply radial deviation to the thumb
  • Indications
    • Increased laxity when compared bilaterally
  • Implications
    • Injury to the 1st UCL of the thumb

Wrist, Hand, and Finger Taping

Wrist Tape

  • Uses: Preventative taping for wrist hyperextension
  • Procedure
    • Position the athlete with fingers extended to contract muscles
    • Begin with 1 ½” tape on the wrist, wrapping three times with complete overlapping
    • Spiral up a couple of times proximally
    • Apply coverstrip or tail with half overlap to finish

Figure 8

  • Uses: Preventative taping for wrist hyperextension
  • Procedure
    • Position the athlete with fingers extended to contract muscles
    • Apply 1 ½” tape anchor on the wrist
    • While moving to the medial aspect of the wrist, begin moving distally to cross over the palm, over the webbing between the thumb and pointer finger, and on the dorsal aspect of the hand
    • Repeat three times then finish with a cover strip

Thumb Tape

  • Uses: Preventative taping for thumb hyperextension
  • Procedure
    • Position the athlete with thumb in slight opposition and with hand rotated (as in a handshake)
    • Begin with 1” tape on the thumb, creating an ‘X’ pattern on the dorsal aspect of the thumb
    • Work down the hand and around the wrist, completely overlapping previous tape
    • Repeat three times
    • Apply cover strip around thumb to finish

Wrist Hyperextension Tape

  • Uses: Preventative taping for wrist hyperextension
  • Procedure
    • Position the athlete’s forearm supinated and in slight flexion
    • Apply two anchors on the forearm and one across the palmar and dorsal aspect
    • Apply three support strips from anchor to anchor
    • Cover strips with an optional figure-8 to reinforce tape job

Buddy Tape

  • Uses: Finger splint, usually for sprains
  • Procedure
    • Identify joint and ensure tape to be applied distal and proximal to joint
    • Use longer finger to tape to - middle finger is either or
    • Do not tape directly over the joint!
    • Rip 1” tape in half and apply two strips without too much tension, wrapping two to three times around the finger

Cranial Nerve Assessment

I. Olfactory (Sensory)

  • Function: Oflaction or smelling
  • Procedure
    • Pick two test items (ex. mint and chocolate) without pt seeing
    • Tell pt to close their eyes for the entirety of the test
    • Ask pt to close one of their nostrils and hold up one of the items to smell, asking if it smells like anything
    • Switch nostrils and repeat with the same item
    • Repeat the two previous steps with the alternative item
    • Record responses from the pt

II. Optic (Sensory)

  • Function: Vision or sight
  • Procedure #1
    • Stand an arm’s length away from the pt and have them cover one eye while looking into provider’s eye
    • Hold specific number of fingers in different parts of the field of vision
    • Ask pt how many fingers are held up and record responses and observations
    • Repeat the test with the other eye
  • Procedure #2
    • Position pt 20 ft away from Snellen Eye Chart
    • Examiner stands next to chart and direct the pt
    • Ask pt to cover one eye and read aloud letters on the lowest line visible
    • Ask pt to continue to read each line until they are unable to make out letters clearly
    • The last line a pt can read while covering one eye determines visual acuity for their other eye
    • Repeat procedure for the other eye and then with both eyes open
    • Record visual acuity

III. Oculomotor (Motor)

  • Function
    • Pupillary reactions
    • Elevation of upper eyelid
    • Eye adduction/abduction
    • Eye elevation/depression
    • Accommodation
  • Procedure #1
    • Ask pt to look at an object over left shoulder with the lights off
    • Using a penlight, measure pt reaction to light by alternating shining the light into eyes and checking each eye individually to reaction
    • Record observations
  • Procedure #2
    • While standing in front of pt, ask pt to follow penlight with their eyes, not their head
    • Slowly trace a rectangle (12” - 18”) in front of them at a rate of 1-2” per second
    • Ensure finger moves far enough out to see all eye movements
    • Stop at each corner for 2 seconds to observe for typical nystagmus
    • Bring penlight directly toward pt’s nose to test accommodation - normal reaction will be if pt looks cross-eyed with pupils constricted
    • Record observations

IV. Trochlear (Motor)

  • Function: Eye depression and adduction
  • Procedure: Same as testing Oculomotor (CN III)

V. Trigeminal (Sensory & Motor)

  • Function: Sensation in the face/head & muscles for chewing
  • Procedure
    • Ask pt to clench their teeth
    • Palpate the strength of their masseter muscle on the cheek, checking bilaterally
    • Apply resistance under the mandible as they attempt to open their mouth
    • Ask pt to close eyes while touching sides of their face, recording all observations

VI. Abducens (Motor)

  • Function: Eye abduction
  • Procedure: Same as testing Oculomotor (CN III)

VII. Facial (Sensory & Motor)

  • Function: Movement of fascial muscles & taste
  • Procedure #1
    • Ask pt to perform tasks such as smiling, frowning, and puffing out both cheeks
    • Record observations below
  • Procedure #2
    • With pt’s eyes closed, place a test item into pt’s mouth
    • Ask if they taste anything, recording response and observations

VIII. Vestibulocochlear (Sensory)

  • Function: Hearing, equilibrium
  • Procedure #1
    • Ask pt to close both eyes throughout the entire procedure
    • Rup fingers around 2” away from pt’s ear, asking what ear they hear the noise in
    • Swap ears and repeat
    • Record observations
  • Procedure #2 (Romberg’s Test)
    • Stand close to the pt to ensure they do not hurt themselves
    • Ask pt to stand with their feet together, arms straight to their sides, and once ready close their eyes
    • Tell pt to hold position for one minute
    • Note inability to hold position and record observations

IX. Glossopharyngeal (Sensory & Motor)

  • Function: Muscles for swallowing & taste
  • Procedure
    • Ask pt to swallow, checking for any abnormalities while doing so
    • Ask pt to open mouth and stick tongue out
    • Place tongue depressor in the center of their tongue and depress the tongue as pt says ‘ah’
    • Check if soft palate for equal elevation on both sides with uvula staying in the midline
    • Use tongue depressor to depress onto posterior tongue to elicit gag reflex

X. Vagus (Sensory & Motor)

  • Function: Muscles for pharynx/larynx, gag reflex & taste
  • Procedure: Same as testing Glossopharyngeal (CN IX)

XI. Spinal Accessory (Motor)

  • Function: Movement of head/shoulder (trapezius and SCM muscles)
  • Procedure #1
    • Ask pt to shrug both soldiers upward while provider resists motion by pushing down on shoulders for 15 seconds
    • Note strength and ability to hold against resistance and record observations
  • Procedure #2
    • Ask pt to look all the way to the right, then place hand on their left cheek and ask pt to move their head left
    • Resist pt’s movement and repeat on the other side
    • Record observations and make note of strength

XII. Hypoglossal

  • Function: Movement of the tongue
  • Procedure
    • Ask pt to stick out their tongue
    • Observe for symmetry and deviation from the midline
    • Then tell pt to move tongue side to side, observing for symmetry
    • Record observations
